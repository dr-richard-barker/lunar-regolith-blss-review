1. Physicochemical & Toxicological Passivation

Section Context & Purpose: This section details the geochemical hazards of raw lunar regolith versus the therapeutic passivation achieved through thermal sintering. Lunar dust contains submicroscopic nanophase metallic iron ($npFe^0$) and unpassivated silicate radicals ($-\text{SiO}^\cdot$, $-\text{Si}^\cdot$) created by space weathering. When exposed to oxygenated habitat water, these features drive Fenton-like reactions ($\text{Fe}^{2+} + \text{H}_2\text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{Fe}^{3+} + \cdot\text{OH} + \text{OH}^-$), producing destructive Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS). Sintering above 1050°C oxidizes $npFe^0$ into stable silicates (augite, olivine) and smooths sharp grain edges, restoring plant transcriptomic homeostasis.

3. 1/6th Gravity Multiphase Fluid Dynamics & Pore Simulator

Section Context & Purpose: Reduced lunar gravity ($g_{luna} = 1.62\text{ m/s}^2$) lowers the dimensionless Bond number ($Bo = \Delta\rho g L^2 / \sigma$), causing capillary forces to dominate over gravitational drainage. In unsorted raw regolith, water is tightly trapped, dropping air-filled porosity below 10% and causing severe root hypoxia. Sieving and sintering regolith into monodisperse pore throat sizes (30–60 µm) stabilizes matric suction ($\psi_m \approx -2.5\text{ to }-5.0\text{ kPa}$) and secures continuous root-zone aeration.

4. Equivalent System Mass (ESM) & Lifecycle Economics

Section Context & Purpose: Equivalent System Mass ($ESM = M + V\cdot C_V + P\cdot C_P + C\cdot C_C + T\cdot C_T$) converts physical mass, pressurized volume, power, thermal cooling, and crew labor into a single metric (kg). Direct regolith farming has a low initial launch mass but high operational consumable penalties (water loss, acid buffering). A sintering plant requires higher initial launch mass (~1,850 kg vs ~420 kg) but achieves breakeven within 18–22 months by eliminating water and buffer resupply burdens.

5. Closed-Loop Ecosystem Architecture & Regeneration

Section Context & Purpose: Sustaining life on the Moon requires linking plant cultivation, saprotrophic fungal bioconversion (mycoponics), microbial bioleaching (BioRock/BioAsteroid), and thermal substrate regeneration into a closed-loop system. Lignocellulosic straw (40–60% of crop biomass) is converted by *Pleurotus ostreatus* on porous ceramic plates into edible protein. Microbial side-reactors extract $\text{K}^+$, $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ from raw regolith, while a 500°C thermal bake-out pyrolyzes root debris, sterilizing and regenerating the ceramic matrix for infinite re-use.
